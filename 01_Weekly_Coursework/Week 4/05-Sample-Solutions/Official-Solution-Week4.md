Official solution — Week 4: Synthesis, thematic coding, and draft literature review

Context and continuity

- Continues from Week 1 topic choices, Week 2 scoping plan, and Week 3 annotated bibliography and extraction table. Week 4 focuses on synthesizing extracted data into themes and producing a draft literature-review narrative or structured outline.

1. Goals for Week 4

- Thematically code extracted results and produce a structured draft literature review (2,000–3,000 words) or a detailed outline with key evidence mapped to each section.

2. Thematic coding approach (model)

- Step A: Familiarization — re-read extracted summaries and highlight recurring concepts.
- Step B: Open coding — assign initial codes (e.g., "dataset limitations", "temporal attention", "field deployment", "evaluation metrics").
- Step C: Axial coding — group codes into candidate themes (e.g., "Modeling approaches", "Datasets & benchmarks", "Deployment challenges", "Evaluation practices").
- Step D: Theme refinement — create theme definitions and list supporting studies.

3. Example thematic map (model)

- Theme: Modeling approaches
  - Codes: transformer adaptations, multimodal fusion, temporal attention
  - Key studies: Smith et al. (2023), Nguyen et al. (2024)

- Theme: Datasets & benchmarks
  - Codes: synthetic datasets, small annotated datasets, lack of construction-specific corpora

- Theme: Deployment challenges
  - Codes: false positives, occlusions, privacy/ethics, integration with sensors

4. Draft literature review outline (model)

- Introduction: topic, scope, and research question
- Methods: brief description of scoping approach, search strategy, inclusion/exclusion
- Results — Descriptive synthesis: corpus characteristics (years, venues, model types, datasets)
- Results — Thematic synthesis: present themes with evidence and exemplar studies
- Discussion: gaps, methodological limitations, implications for practice and research
- Conclusion: summary and clear directions for next steps

5. Writing guidance (short)

- Use evidence to support claims: cite extracted data and metrics.
- Maintain structure: each theme should open with a summary sentence then evidence and a short synthesis paragraph.
- Address limitations explicitly and suggest targeted future work (e.g., larger, annotated construction datasets; field trials; standardized metrics).

6. Week 4 marking rubric (50 points)

- Thematic synthesis quality — 20 pts: Coherent themes, accurate mapping to literature, and insightful synthesis (20).
- Argument and structure — 10 pts: Logical flow and clear linkage to research question (10).
- Use of evidence — 10 pts: Correct citations, metrics, and balanced interpretation (10).
- Writing clarity & formatting — 10 pts: Professional writing, references, and appendices (10).

7. Instructor notes

- Accept either a full draft (2k–3k words) or a detailed outline with strong evidence mapping if students are short on time.
- Encourage students to include a PRISMA flow diagram or equivalent corpus summary figure in appendices.
