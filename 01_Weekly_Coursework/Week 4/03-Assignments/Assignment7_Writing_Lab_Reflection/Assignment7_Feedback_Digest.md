## Methodology Guidance For Chapter Three

The descriptive analysis should be frequentist and mapping-oriented, not a meta-analysis. The chapter should describe the body of studies and the review process rather than trying to compute pooled effects.

Recommended descriptive categories:

- Number of included studies.
- Publication years and publication trends.
- Countries, regions, or geographic settings represented in the studies.
- Journals and types of publication.
- Study characteristics, including research design, methodology, data sources, sample population, sample size, and setting.
- Topic and content characteristics, including outcomes examined and what each study considered.
- Methodological characteristics, including modeling approaches, statistical techniques, machine-learning methods, survey methods, logistic models, and cross-sectional designs.
- Theories or conceptual frameworks used in the studies, including whether the studies lack explicit theoretical grounding.
- Frequent research methodologies and interventions appearing across the literature.

## Structural Guidance For Findings

The findings should not be repeated across two chapters. The better structure is one findings chapter that reports the mapped patterns and thematic results in one place. Discussion or interpretation can be integrated after each major finding or placed in a later discussion section without restating the same evidence.
