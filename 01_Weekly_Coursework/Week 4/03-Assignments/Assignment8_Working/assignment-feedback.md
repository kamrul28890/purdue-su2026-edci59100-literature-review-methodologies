MD, 

You are making strong progress here. I can see the careful thinking behind how you are organizing the literature, especially in how you are tracing the movement from earlier computer vision approaches into more recent VLM-based work. You are doing more than listing studies, and you are starting to show how ideas build over time. At this stage, the next step is to make those connections a bit more visible and consistent, so the synthesis comes through more clearly as you move across your matrix and into your themes.

Here are my observations and suggestions:

Your matrix is well organized, and I can follow how you are grouping studies around meaningful transitions in the field. One thing that stands out is how you are consistently noting how each study connects to earlier work. That’s exactly the kind of thinking you want at this stage.

To strengthen this further, it will help to make the synthesis more present within each entry rather than letting it sit primarily in the “connection” column. Right now, many of your entries describe what the study does and then explain how it connects. You might try bringing those ideas together a bit more.

For example, instead of separating the description and the connection, you could combine them into something like:
→ This study builds on earlier activity-recognition work but begins to shift toward interpretation, connecting visual evidence to safety meaning rather than only detecting tasks.

That way, the comparison is doing more of the work directly in the entry.

Along with that, you are already identifying important limitations (e.g., task-specific models, reliance on static images, weak temporal reasoning), which is great. As you continue refining, it will help to more explicitly show how those limitations repeat or shift across studies.

For example:
→ Earlier models achieve strong accuracy in controlled settings, but across studies they struggle to capture broader safety context, which later VLM-based approaches attempt to address.

Your categories (baseline, transition, core VLM, progress/benchmark) are working well as an organizing structure, and I can see the overall trajectory you are building—from detection to reasoning and interpretation.

As you continue refining, it may help to make that progression even more explicit in how you frame your themes. Right now, the structure is there, but you can push it a bit further, so each theme clearly represents a stage in how the field is evolving.

For instance:

one stage focuses on recognizing objects and tasks
another on interpreting safety meaning
and another on integrating reasoning, reporting, and interaction
Making those stages more explicit will strengthen your overall argument.

Your organization is also clear in how you are using the “gap or use in outline” column. I can see how you are already thinking ahead to how each source will function in your paper, which is a strong approach.

To refine this further, you might start shifting your language so those “uses” read a bit more like claims about the literature rather than placement decisions.

For example:
→ instead of “use in Theme 2 to show movement…”
you might phrase it as
→ This work reflects a shift from PPE detection toward contextual hazard interpretation.

This will help your outline translate more smoothly into writing later.

Based on my review, I suggest focusing on:

Make your synthesis more visible within each entry – combining description and comparison so connections are clear as you read across the matrix
Strengthen the sense of progression across themes – clarifying how the field moves from detection to interpretation and reasoning
Refine how you frame study “uses” – shifting from placement language to analytical statements about what the literature shows
Best,
