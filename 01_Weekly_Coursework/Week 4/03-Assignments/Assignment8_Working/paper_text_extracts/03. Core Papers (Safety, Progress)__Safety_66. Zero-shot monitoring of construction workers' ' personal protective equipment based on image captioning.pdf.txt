Keywords:                                                   Previous studies have deployed object-detection-based approaches to automate personal protective equipment
Zero-shot detection                                         (PPE) safety monitoring. However, previously proposed methods require large amounts of labeled data for
Computer vision                                             different PPE items. This study proposes a zero-shot PPE monitoring method that does not require a training
Site monitoring
                                                            process to overcome this problem. The proposed method comprises three steps. First, it detects workers onsite
Image captioning
Human-pose estimation

                                                            text using image captioning. Finally, the extracted text is compared with prompts describing body parts wearing
                                                            PPE, and safety is determined based on cosine similarity. Compared to the F1-score of 73.5% achieved by

3. Proposed method

    In the initial attempt to detect PPE use by workers, CLIP was fully
applied to the entire process with only text prompts because this method
was designed for text-based ZSL. However, the proposed approach did
not successfully produce acceptable performance in detecting detailed
objects, such as hardhats, via simple label transfer. Therefore, a target-
specific approach was attempted while maintaining a large framework
in a similar manner. After the caption text was extracted from the target-
specific–body-part images, text embedding via a language model was
applied to determine whether the PPE was worn. Therefore, the pro­
posed zero-shot PPE monitoring system primarily comprises three steps:
1) body-part extraction using pose estimation, 2) image captioning, and
