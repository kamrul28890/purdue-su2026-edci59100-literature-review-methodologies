Keywords:                                                 Recently, vision-based methods have been widely used to analyze the construction productivity based on onsite
Vision-based method                                       videos owing to their low cost, simple deployment, and easy maintenance. However, existing vision-based
Earthmoving projects                                      methods rely on supervised learning for activity recognition, which is computationally intensive owing to the
Productivity analysis
                                                          necessity of labeling large-scale training datasets. To address this problem, this paper describes a vision-based
Zero-shot learning
Activity recognition
                                                          method for automatically analyzing excavators' productivities in earthmoving tasks by adopting zero-shot
                                                          learning for activity recognition. The proposed method can identify activities of general construction ma­
                                                          chines (e.g., excavators and loaders) without pre-training or fine-tuning. To verify the feasibility, the proposed
                                                          method has been tested on videos recorded from real construction sites. The accuracy values for activity
                                                          recognition and productivity evaluation are 86% and 87.8%, respectively.

Appendix A. Experiments for threshold selection

Threshold selection for majority voting

    In the majority voting process, the activity recognition results of all frames are fed into the majority voting algorithm. Then, the algorithm is
implemented with a threshold of 2/3 for activity recognition. In other words, the activity recognized in two-thirds of the frames is regarded as the
activity of the video. In the existing studies, the majority voting method has been used for smoothing the activity recognition results. In general, if over
half the frames correspond to the same activity, the video considered to correspond the major activity. When this strategy is applied in this study, an
accuracy of approximately 100% is obtained. However, in several clips, the number of two activities is identical, which means that the activity
recognition module is sensitive. To overcome this issue, an experiment is conducted to explore the use of different thresholds. Based on the results
presented in Table A1, a threshold of 2/3 is selected.
