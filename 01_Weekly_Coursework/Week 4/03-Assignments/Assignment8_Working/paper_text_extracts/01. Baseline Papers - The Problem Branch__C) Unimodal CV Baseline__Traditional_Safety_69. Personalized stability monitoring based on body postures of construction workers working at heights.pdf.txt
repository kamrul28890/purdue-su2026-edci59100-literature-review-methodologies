Stability monitoring                                          heights. Thus, stability monitoring of workers is important for proactive accident prevention. However, workers’

Working at height

                                                              has been paid to the personalized posture-based stability analysis of workers working at heights. This study
                                                              proposes a personalized stability monitoring framework based on workers’ body posture patterns when working
                                                              at heights. The proposed method includes two main components: posture recognition and stability monitoring.
                                                              First, OpenPose is used to extract the coordinates of workers’ posture key points from video clips. Two posture
                                                              features, duration and count, are extracted to reflect workload and frequency. Secondly, a two-stage stability
                                                              monitoring framework, including instability detection and instability evaluation, is designed based on the
                                                              Gaussian model and Gaussian mixture model. Finally, the proposed framework is validated by on-site con­
                                                              struction videos of workers working at height. The validation results showed the accuracy of 84.38% and the
                                                              precision of 81.25% in identifying the stable status of a subject. The robustness of the personalised monitoring
                                                              method was validated through comparisons with five other workers. The study provides a practical reference for
                                                              active safety monitoring for workers with high fall-from-height risk. It also helps to extend personalized and
                                                              adaptive behaviour-based safety training for construction workers.

3. Methodology

3.1. Research framework

    Fig. 1 illustrates the framework for monitoring the stability of con­
struction workers working at height. The framework includes five
modules: skeleton detection, posture identification, feature extraction,
personalized stability monitoring, instability detection, and validation.
Skeleton detection is achieved through computer vision techniques.
Compared to other skeleton extraction techniques (e.g., wearable sen­
sors), this method has the advantage of being cost-effective and does not
cause additional disruption to operations at height (Chian et al., 2021;
MassirisFernández et al., 2020). Firstly, workers’ skeleton and relative
joint coordinates are detected based on images extracted from video
frames. Postures that can reflect the stability of workers and behaviours
are identified based on joint-related information. The time and duration
of the posture are recorded based on the real-time feature extraction
algorithm. Secondly, the collected features are imported into the
personalized stability monitoring module and fed into a Gaussian model
(GM) and a Gaussian mixture model (GMM) to calculate workers’
personalized threshold for two-stage instability monitoring of stability
detection and evaluation. Finally, the stability of worker is obtained by
comparing his pose sequence features with the thresholds set. Other
workers’ pose sequence features are collected and compared with the
initial worker’s personalised threshold to analyse the robustness of the
personalized method. Details of the modules, processes, and algorithms
are illustrated in the following sections.
