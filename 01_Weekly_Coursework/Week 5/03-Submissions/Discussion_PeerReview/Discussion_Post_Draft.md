## Discussion Post (to paste into D2L)

My draft is a scoping review of Vision-Language Models applied to construction site safety and progress monitoring — 56 papers, PRISMA-ScR protocol, full submission attached.

The backbone of the paper is a taxonomy I built called the Three-Layered Spatiotemporal VLM Framework. Layer 1 is sensor/input, Layer 2 is the cognitive engine (I broke that into three chronological paradigms — zero-shot retrieval, domain fine-tuned generative, and hybrid agentic systems), and Layer 3 is the construction management output. The structure works well for tracking how the field evolved: from CLIP doing similarity matching on PPE images, to transformer-based captioning generating daily site reports, to YOLO + LLM stacks doing real-time safety compliance checks with regulation ontologies. That progression feels tight and the paradigm organization genuinely helped me fit 56 papers into a coherent argument rather than a laundry list.

The piece I'm least confident about is Chapter 3 — the descriptive analysis. I have year-by-year publication counts, a PRISMA flow diagram, geographic breakdown, journal landscape table, and a 25-paper master charting table (all Phase 1 studies: 20 core VLM + 5 transition). But I wrote that section from scratch without a close model to compare against. I don't know whether the level of analytical commentary I'm providing on top of the numbers is adequate for a scoping review, or whether it reads more like a raw statistics dump.

Two specific things I'd like feedback on:

## Peer Review Response Template (when reviewing classmates)

**Structure to use (Praise / Question / Suggest):**

**Praise:** [1–2 specific things that work — reference actual section or argument, not generic compliments]

**Question:** [1 genuine interpretive question — something I actually don't understand or want to know more about]

**Suggest:** [1 concrete, actionable revision suggestion with a rationale]

## Notes on Tone

- Post reads as someone who knows the work well and has real, specific questions — not a generic "here's my paper" entry
- Two feedback requests are targeted, not open-ended ("what do you think of my paper?")
- Avoids: "I'd like to share," "In conclusion," "delve into," "fascinating," triple hedge stacking, perfectly parallel bullet lists
- Connects the reviewer to the actual content (paradigm taxonomy, RQ structure) so they can give meaningful responses
