That's the justification for a scoping (rather than systematic) review. Does this justification land convincingly, or does a skeptical reader need more explicit evidence — e.g., a brief survey of what prior reviews actually omit?

**Q4 — Limitations section length:**

Is that brevity appropriate for a scoping review, or do reviewers expect a full subsection with more elaboration on each limitation?

**Q5 — Findings chapter coherence across paradigm/domain split:**
The paper splits thematic analysis across two chapters: Chapter 4 covers VLM architectures (the "how" — three paradigms), Chapter 5 covers construction applications (the "what" — safety vs. progress). The same 20 papers appear in both chapters from different analytical angles. Does this structure read as coherent double coverage, or does it feel repetitive? Should I consider merging them with explicit cross-referencing instead?
