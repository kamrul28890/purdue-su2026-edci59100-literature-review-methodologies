### Step 2: Review the Analysis Framework
Before analyzing each article, review the clues for identifying methodologies:

| Look For... | Indicates This Type |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **Broad, exploratory question** | Narrative Review |
| **Pattern-seeking across studies** | Integrative Review |
| **Theory development/analysis** | Theoretical Review |
| **Mapping literature landscape** | Scoping Review |
| **Specific focused question** | Systematic Review |

| Look For... | Indicates This Type |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **Implicit/flexible search** | Narrative or Integrative |
| **Explicitly stated search strategy** | Systematic Review |
| **Structured but flexible** | Scoping Review |
| **Concept-driven search** | Theoretical Review |

### Step 3: Analyze Article 1 - Evidence and Gap Maps (Saran et al.)

#### What to Look For:
1. **Research Question**: What is the article trying to accomplish?
   - Is it broad or narrow?
   - Is it about identifying gaps or answering a specific question?

2. **Search Strategy**: How did they find articles?
   - Does the article describe their search process?
   - Is it systematic and reproducible?
   - Is it flexible and adaptive?

3. **Inclusion Criteria**: How did they decide what to include?
   - Are criteria explicitly stated?
   - Are they strict or flexible?
   - Do they focus on specific types of evidence?

4. **Literature Sources**: What types of articles did they include?
   - Only empirical studies?
   - Mix of theory and empirical?
   - Gray literature included?

5. **Analysis/Synthesis**: How did they analyze findings?
   - Thematic summary?
   - Pattern identification?
   - Mapping and categorization?
   - Structured coding?

#### Prediction Process:
1. Read the abstract and introduction
2. Identify the main purpose
3. Look for the methods section (if present)
4. Note any data about their search strategy
5. Identify their conclusion/contribution
6. Make your prediction based on evidence

#### Document Your Analysis:
In the assignment document, record:
- **Your Prediction**: Narrative / Integrative / Theoretical / Scoping / Systematic
- **Key Evidence**: Quote 1-2 specific passages that led to your prediction
- **Your Reasoning**: In 2-3 sentences, explain why this methodology fits

**Example Format**:
> **Article 1 Analysis**
> 
> **Predicted Methodology**: Scoping Review
> 
> **Key Evidence**: 
> - "This paper provides a comprehensive review of different approaches to evidence and gap mapping..."
> - "We systematically compared methodologies used in 47 published gap maps..."
> 
> **Reasoning**: The article's purpose is to map the landscape of evidence and gap mapping approaches (scoping characteristic). The search appears systematic and comprehensive. The outcome is a categorized overview of different methodologies, which is typical of scoping reviews.

# ASSIGNMENT 2: Topic Exploration and Initial Direction

## Assignment Goal
Systematically explore potential research topics, evaluate their feasibility, and select one direction to move forward with for your literature review.

## What You're Being Graded On
✓ **Effort and completeness** - Did you thoroughly explore ideas?
✓ **Clarity of ideas** - Can readers understand your topics?
✓ **Thoughtfulness of analysis** - Did you seriously consider scope and feasibility?
✓ **Application of course concepts** - Did you reference what you learned about methodologies?

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### Step 1: Brainstorm 2-3 Topic Ideas

#### Consider Your Research Interests
Ask yourself:
- What problems interest me in my field?
- What questions do I want answered?
- What would I enjoy researching for 6 weeks?
- What topics connect to my dissertation/thesis?
- What current issues in my field need better understanding?

#### Generate Broad Topic Ideas
Don't filter yet—just brainstorm 2-3 topics that genuinely interest you. Examples:

- **Education**: How do multigenerational classrooms affect learning outcomes?
- **Technology**: What barriers prevent technology adoption in higher education?
- **Management**: How do organizations balance innovation with risk management?
- **Healthcare**: What factors influence patient adherence to treatment protocols?
- **Environmental**: How effective are community-based sustainability initiatives?

#### Document Your Initial Ideas
For each broad topic, write 1-2 sentences:
> **Topic 1: [Topic Name]**
> 
> *Description*: Brief description of the topic and why it interests you.

### Step 2: Evaluate Scope and Feasibility for Each Topic

For each of your 2-3 topics, complete this analysis:

#### Is the Scope Appropriate?

**TOO BROAD** ❌ (Avoid)
- "Everything about technology in education"
- "All aspects of organizational management"
- "How culture affects everything"

**APPROPRIATE** ✓ (Good)
- "How learning management systems impact student engagement in online courses"
- "Strategies for managing innovation risk in small manufacturing firms"
- "Cultural factors affecting healthcare provider-patient communication"

**TOO NARROW** ⚠️ (Might not have enough research)
- "How [specific software] was used by [specific university] in [specific year]"
- "The effect of [one specific intervention] on [one specific outcome]"

#### Does Sufficient Literature Likely Exist?

Ask yourself:
- Is this topic discussed in academic literature?
- Would major databases (PubMed, ERIC, Business Source Complete, etc.) have articles?
- Is there a research community studying this?
- Are there at least 10-20 relevant articles you can find? (You need 20+ for a 6-week review)

Check feasibility by:
1. Do a quick **sample search** in Google Scholar or your library database
2. Try 2-3 search term combinations
3. See if you get reasonable results
4. If you find 0 articles = too narrow/niche
5. If you find 1000+ = might be too broad

#### Can You Complete This in 6 Weeks?

Realistic assessment:
- Do you have time and resources?
- Is the scope manageable?
- Will you need to learn new concepts/methodologies?
- Do you have access to the literature?

#### Document Your Analysis

For each topic, create a feasibility analysis table:

| Aspect | Analysis | Rating |
|--------|----------|--------|
| **Scope Appropriateness** | Is it focused but not too narrow? | Too Broad / Good / Too Narrow |
| **Literature Availability** | Will you find enough studies? | Abundant / Adequate / Scarce |
| **Time Feasibility** | Can you complete in 6 weeks? | Yes / Possibly / No |
| **Interest Level** | How interested are you (1-10)? | [Your rating] |
| **Field Relevance** | How relevant to your field? | High / Medium / Low |
| **Overall Feasibility** | Should you pursue this? | Strong / Moderate / Weak |

### Step 3: Initial Methodology Thinking (Exploratory)

**For each topic**, consider: *What type of literature review might fit?*

**Example Analysis**:

> **Topic: Impact of Technology on Student Engagement**
> 
> *Possible Methodologies*:
> - **Scoping Review** could map the landscape of research on technology-student engagement relationships (many studies exist)
> - **Integrative Review** could synthesize findings across different technologies, student populations, and engagement measures
> - **Systematic Review** would be appropriate if we had a specific question like "What is the effect of [specific tech] on engagement scores?"
> 
> *Initial Thinking*: An integrative review seems most appropriate because we want to understand patterns across diverse technology implementations and student populations.

**Note**: You're NOT committing to a methodology yet—this is exploratory thinking to apply course concepts.

### Step 4: Select Your Strongest Topic

Choose ONE topic to move forward with. Criteria for selection:
1. ✓ Adequate scope (not too broad, not too narrow)
2. ✓ Sufficient literature likely exists
3. ✓ Feasible to complete in 6 weeks
4. ✓ Genuinely interests you
5. ✓ Relevant to your field/research goals

#### Explain Your Rationale

Write 2-3 sentences explaining WHY you chose this topic:

> I selected "Teacher Professional Development in Online Teaching" because (1) it directly connects to my dissertation focus on online education, (2) significant literature exists on both professional development AND online teaching, allowing for synthesis of patterns, (3) this is an emerging area where a literature review would help clarify current practices and identify gaps, and (4) understanding this topic will directly inform my research direction.
