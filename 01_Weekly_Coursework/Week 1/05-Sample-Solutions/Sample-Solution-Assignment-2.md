### 2. Selected Topic Selection

**Final Selected Topic:** Promoting Educational Equity through Universal Design for Learning (UDL) in Multigenerational Online Classrooms.

**Why this topic?**
As classrooms become increasingly diverse in terms of age and technological fluency (multigenerational), traditional "one-size-fits-all" instructional methods may inadvertently create barriers. This topic is significant because it explores how a specific framework (UDL) can be applied to ensure all learners, regardless of their generational background or technical experience, have equitable access to learning opportunities.

### 4. Initial Reflection

**Initial Goal/Interest:**
My goal is to identify specific instructional strategies within the UDL framework that most effectively bridge the gap between "Digital Natives" and "Digital Immigrants" in online graduate-level courses.

**Anticipated Challenges:**
One challenge will be the "fuzzy" definitions of generational cohorts in the literature (e.g., Millennials vs. Gen Z). Another challenge will be finding empirical studies that specifically measure the *equity* impact of UDL across different age groups, as many studies focus on UDL for disabilities rather than generational diversity.

**How this aligns with course concepts:**
This topic is well-suited for an **Integrative Review** because it will require synthesizing theoretical frameworks of UDL with empirical data on student engagement and generational studies. It allows for the development of a conceptual model for "Generational-Inclusive Design."
