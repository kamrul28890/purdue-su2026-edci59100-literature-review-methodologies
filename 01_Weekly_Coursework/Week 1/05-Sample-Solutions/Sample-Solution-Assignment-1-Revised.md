## PART 2: DETAILED EXPLANATIONS FOR EACH ARTICLE

### Article 1: Saran et al. (2018) - Evidence and Gap Maps

**Review Type: Scoping Review**

**Evidence Supporting Classification:**

The primary evidence for classifying this as a **Scoping Review** is:

1. **Mapping Purpose**: The article's core purpose is to map the landscape of "evidence and gap mapping approaches" rather than synthesize evidence to answer a clinical question or develop theory. The authors are cataloging and categorizing different methodologies in the field.

2. **Broad, Systematic Search**: While the search is systematic and comprehensive (identifying all published gap maps), the goal is breadth of coverage and categorization, not depth of synthesis. Scoping reviews characteristically aim to provide a comprehensive overview of what research exists and how it is structured.

3. **Methodology Focus**: The primary outcome is a categorized framework showing different approaches to evidence and gap mapping—a typical scoping review output. The authors identify variations in methodology, not evidence answers or theoretical insights.

4. **Research Question Structure**: The question "What are the different approaches to evidence and gap mapping?" is inherently exploratory and landscape-mapping in nature, which is the hallmark of scoping reviews.

---

### Article 2: Keupp & Gassmann (2009) - International Entrepreneurship

**Review Type: Systematic Review**

**Evidence Supporting Classification:**

The primary evidence for classifying this as a **Systematic Review** is:

1. **Explicit Systematic Methodology**: The authors explicitly state they "systematically reviewed 342 articles" using a "rigorous search process." This language directly indicates structured, transparent, reproducible methodology—core features of systematic reviews.

2. **Narrow, Focused Question**: The review asks a specific, focused question within the international entrepreneurship field: "What does the literature show?" This is narrower than narrative or integrative reviews and typical of systematic reviews.

3. **Predefined Search Strategy**: The systematic process over a "14-year period" indicates predefined temporal and conceptual boundaries, with likely explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria applied consistently across all 342 articles.

4. **Field Synthesis Goal**: The outcome is a synthesized answer to a field-specific question with clear identification of trends and future research directions—not exploratory mapping or theory development.

5. **Rigor and Transparency**: The language and methodology description emphasize reproducibility and explicit procedures, distinguishing systematic reviews from less formal approaches.

---

### Article 3: Byrne et al. (2020) - Person-Centred Care in Nursing

**Review Type: Integrative Review**

**Evidence Supporting Classification:**

The primary evidence for classifying this as an **Integrative Review** is:

1. **Explicit Self-Identification**: The title literally states "An integrative review," removing ambiguity about methodology choice.

2. **Intentional Integration of Diverse Sources**: The article explicitly combines empirical research, theoretical literature, and conceptual papers. Integrative reviews are specifically designed to synthesize across different evidence types, which is exactly what occurs here.

3. **Complex Concept Clarification**: The goal is to clarify and develop a comprehensive understanding of "person-centred care"—a complex concept that cannot be answered by empirical studies alone. Integrative reviews excel at synthesizing diverse perspectives to advance conceptual understanding.

4. **Mixed Method Sources**: Inclusion of both quantitative and qualitative empirical studies plus theoretical works indicates the hallmark integrative review structure of bringing together different methodological approaches.

5. **Pattern-Finding Across Diversity**: The outcome is not narrow evidence synthesis or field mapping, but rather advanced understanding through integrating different types of knowledge—the defining characteristic of integrative reviews.

---

### Article 4: Lowell & Morris (2019) - Multigenerational Classrooms

**Review Type: Narrative Review**

**Evidence Supporting Classification:**

The primary evidence for classifying this as a **Narrative Review** is:

1. **Explicit Self-Identification**: The authors explicitly state they "conducted a narrative review," clearly indicating methodology choice.

2. **Flexible, Interpretative Approach**: Rather than describing a formal, structured search protocol, the authors describe synthesizing "current findings" to provide an overview. The approach is flexible and author-driven rather than protocol-based.

3. **Broad, Exploratory Question**: The question "What does the literature tell us?" is exploratory and broad, typical of narrative reviews. It seeks understanding of a complex phenomenon rather than answering a specific research question.

4. **Thematic Synthesis**: The focus on identifying "themes and patterns" in current knowledge suggests thematic synthesis—the characteristic analysis method of narrative reviews.

5. **No Formal Methodology Protocol**: Unlike systematic or scoping reviews, there is no description of explicit search strategies, predefined inclusion criteria, or formal data extraction procedures.

---

### Article 5: Price & Oliver (2007) - Technology Impact Framework

**Review Type: Theoretical Review**

**Evidence Supporting Classification:**

The primary evidence for classifying this as a **Theoretical Review** is:

1. **Framework Development**: The title emphasizes "A Framework for Conceptualising," indicating the primary goal is developing a new theoretical framework—the hallmark of theoretical reviews.

2. **Theory-Driven Focus**: The article moves "beyond simple 'success/failure' metrics" to develop sophisticated conceptual understanding. This advancement of theoretical thinking is characteristic of theoretical reviews.

3. **Conceptual Analysis, Not Evidence Synthesis**: Rather than synthesizing empirical findings, the authors analyze and synthesize theoretical concepts to develop new ways of thinking about technology's impact—a theoretical review characteristic.

4. **Critique and Refinement of Existing Concepts**: The approach of analyzing how existing frameworks fall short and proposing superior conceptualizations is typical of theoretical review methodology.

5. **Primary Outcome**: The deliverable is a new conceptual model/framework that advances theoretical understanding in the field, rather than empirical evidence synthesis or landscape mapping.

## PART 3: REFLECTION QUESTIONS

### Question 1: What was most challenging about identifying the methodology?

**Response:**

The most challenging aspect of identifying methodologies was distinguishing between reviews that use **systematic processes but different purposes**. For example, both the Keupp & Gassmann (2009) systematic review and the Saran et al. (2018) scoping review employ structured, rigorous search processes. However, the key difference lies in their ultimate purpose: the systematic review aims to synthesize evidence to answer a field-specific question, while the scoping review aims to map and categorize the landscape of different methodological approaches.

Additionally, articles that use similar language required careful analysis. The Lowell & Morris (2019) narrative review uses the word "synthesize," which also appears in descriptions of integrative and systematic reviews. The challenge was recognizing that synthesis itself is not the distinguishing feature—rather, the **type and integration of sources** and **rigor of search procedures** differentiate methodologies.

---

### Question 2: What patterns did you notice across the five articles?

**Response:**

Several important patterns emerged:

1. **Explicitness of Methodology**: Articles that explicitly name their methodology in the title or abstract (Byrne's "integrative review," Lowell & Morris's "narrative review") were easier to classify. Articles that don't explicitly state methodology require closer examination of methods and purpose.

2. **Source Diversity as a Key Indicator**: 
   - **Integrative reviews** (Byrne) intentionally combine empirical + theoretical sources
   - **Systematic and scoping reviews** (Keupp, Saran) focus primarily on empirical literature
   - **Narrative reviews** (Lowell & Morris) use flexible, mixed sources
   - **Theoretical reviews** (Price & Oliver) focus on conceptual/theoretical sources

3. **Question Specificity and Scope**:
   - **Narrow + Specific questions** → Systematic reviews (Keupp)
   - **Narrow + Theory-focused questions** → Theoretical reviews (Price & Oliver)
   - **Broad + Exploratory questions** → Narrative reviews (Lowell & Morris)
   - **Broad + Mapping questions** → Scoping reviews (Saran)
   - **Broad + Pattern-seeking questions** → Integrative reviews (Byrne)

4. **Search Process Formality**:
   - Highly formal, structured, and transparent = Systematic review
   - Systematic but flexible = Scoping review
   - Flexible and less formally documented = Narrative review
   - Theory-driven and concept-focused = Theoretical review
   - Intentionally diverse = Integrative review

Beyond the explicit statement, the article's characteristics perfectly match the integrative review definition:
- It intentionally combines empirical and theoretical sources
- The goal is to clarify a complex concept (person-centred care)
- The analysis synthesizes across different evidence types
- The outcome is advanced conceptual understanding

When an article self-identifies as a review type AND exhibits characteristics matching that type, classification becomes straightforward.

### Question 4: Which article was most difficult to classify and why?

**Response:**

**Article 1 (Saran et al., 2018) was the most difficult to classify** because its structured, rigorous search methodology could initially suggest a **systematic review**. The authors "systematically compared methodologies used in published gap maps," which uses language typical of systematic reviews.

However, distinguishing features revealed it as a **scoping review**:

1. **Purpose Distinction**: The goal is not to answer a clinical/empirical question, but to **map and categorize different approaches** to evidence and gap mapping—a landscape mapping function.

2. **Question Type**: The question "What are the different approaches?" is exploratory and categorization-focused, not hypothesis-testing or evidence-synthesizing.

3. **Outcome Type**: The result is a structured overview of methodological variations, not evidence-based conclusions about an empirical question.

This confusion highlights that **rigor of process alone does not determine review type**—the fundamental purpose, question structure, and outcome type are equally important classifying factors.
