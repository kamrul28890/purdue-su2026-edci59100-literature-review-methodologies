### Part 2: Reflection Questions

**1. Which methodology seems most challenging to implement? Why?**
The **Systematic Review** is likely the most challenging due to its requirement for extreme rigor, transparency, and reproducibility. It requires a highly specific research question, a pre-defined protocol (like PRISMA), and exhaustive searches across multiple databases, often requiring two or more independent reviewers to minimize bias.

**2. How do the goals of an Integrative Review differ from a Systematic Review?**
A **Systematic Review** typically aims to answer a narrow, specific empirical question (e.g., "Does X intervention improve Y outcome?") by synthesizing high-quality empirical data. In contrast, an **Integrative Review** has a broader goal of synthesizing diverse sources (including empirical, theoretical, and methodological literature) to provide a comprehensive understanding of a phenomenon or to develop new frameworks.

**3. Why might a researcher choose a Scoping Review over a Narrative Review?**
A researcher would choose a **Scoping Review** when they want to formally and systematically map the "landscape" of a topic, especially to identify the types of evidence available and where gaps exist. While both provide overviews, a Scoping Review follows a more structured and transparent process (mapping) than a **Narrative Review**, which is more descriptive and interpretative in nature.
