My post in the discussion:
Hey folks, great to start our six weeks journey together! Here are a couple of topics that I have been thinking about lately:

Idea 1: Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Automated Construction Site Monitoring

Description: Researching how vision-language models can be used to address spatiotemporal reasoning on construction sites.

Why it's important: There is nothing but dynamism and complexity when talking about construction sites. What interests me most is an interesting intersection of AI and construction management – moving away from simple action recognition to the application of advanced vision-language models for analysis of space and time within construction management sites.

What I want to explore: Map existing frameworks to understand how far are we and where can we go with respect to limitations associated with VLM usage.

Idea 2: LLMs for Evaluating Inclusiveness in Educational Material and Official Document

Description: Literature review to see how Large Language Models are used to assess inclusive nature of education material and official documents.

Why it's important: I've done some research looking at educational inclusiveness in textbooks. The emergence of Large Language Models makes this task more automated and important. At the same time, there is always room for improvement and more applications in this field, including the policy and rules.

What I want to explore: Understand existing ways to prompt or train models in such way that they can detect biases and what benchmarks they use. Understand how data are annotated to provide maximum accuracy.

Idea 3: AI and Machine Learning in Construction Workforce Systems

Description: This is a broader look at how machine learning affects construction workforce management.

Why it's important: Labor and workforce management play a big role in construction projects, and the use of AI is increasing. However, there is still much to explore and adjust.  

What I want to explore: I want to see how predictive modeling and data analytics are currently used for workforce safety, scheduling, and resource allocation.

What I’m leaning toward: I am definitely leaning toward the first idea, VLMs for spatiotemporal reasoning in construction. It fits best with my main research goals right now.

Challenges and uncertainties: My biggest challenge will be finding the right search string and strict inclusion criteria. When searching for anything related to computer vision in construction, the results often get cluttered with generic deep learning or basic action recognition papers. I need to narrow my focus to make sure that Vision-Language Models are a required part of the studies. Otherwise, I might end up sifting through thousands of irrelevant articles, which would be too broad for a six-week course.

I would love to hear your thoughts, especially if anyone has advice on filtering out the noise in AI-related literature searches.

Hi Rishika,

Thanks for the great questions! To answer your first point about the current standard, it actually uses a mix of both images and videos, depending heavily on the specific application. Right now, most commercial applications rely on static images (like weekly drone flights or site walkthroughs with 360-degree cameras) to track overall physical progress and compare it against the 3D project model. On the other hand, live video feeds are typically used for real-time safety monitoring, such as detecting if workers are wearing proper personal protective equipment.

This application really started taking off over the last five to seven years with the boom of deep learning and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). However, the current standard is mostly limited to basic object detection, like identifying an excavator or a hardhat, which is exactly why I am looking into Vision-Language Models. The industry is trying to shift toward understanding the actual sequence of events and spatial relationships over time, rather than just identifying isolated objects in a single frame. Highlighting that exact transition from basic detection to complex spatiotemporal reasoning is definitely where a strong publication position lies!

As for my second idea, your hunch is correct, it is more like a passion project. It's not even a part of my PhD journey, just an individual research project I am interested about.
