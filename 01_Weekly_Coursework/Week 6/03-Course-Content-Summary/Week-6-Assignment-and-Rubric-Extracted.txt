Final Literature Review Manuscript
Final Assignment Instructions and Rubric
Assignment Description
This assignment is the culmination of your manuscript development work throughout the course. You will submit a revised and refined literature review manuscript that reflects your selected review methodology, demonstrates synthesis and organization of the literature, and shows thoughtful revision based on feedback and self-assessment.
Because students in this course are using different literature review methodologies (e.g., narrative, integrative, scoping, systematic, theoretical/conceptual), your final manuscript should reflect the current stage of your review process. You are not expected to have completed every stage of a full literature review manuscript. Instead, your submission should represent your strongest, most developed version of the manuscript at this point in the course.
Your final manuscript should demonstrate your ability to:
Organize and synthesize literature in a coherent way
Align structure and analysis with your selected review methodology
Communicate your ideas clearly to a scholarly audience
Develop or begin developing discussion, implication, limitation, and next steps sections as appropriate
Revise your work based on feedback and self-assessment
Required Components
Core Sections Required for All Students
Introduction: Clearly establishes the topic, purpose, and direction of the review.
Methodology / Approach: Explains how the literature was selected, organized, analyzed, mapped, or interpreted, as appropriate to the selected review type.
Organized Literature Review Body: Presents the literature through themes, categories, concepts, findings, mapped areas, or another methodology-aligned structure. This section should demonstrate synthesis rather than source-by-source summary.
Reference Section: Includes a minimum of 20 scholarly sources, cited and formatted consistently.
Developing Functions — Flexible Based on Methodology and Stage
The following elements should be represented in your manuscript in ways that align with your methodology and current stage of development. These may appear as separate sections or be integrated throughout your writing:
Interpretation of the literature (e.g., patterns, themes, or key insights)
Implications or contributions of the review
Awareness of limitations or scope of the review
Required New Element: Next Steps Section
All manuscripts must include a Next Steps component.
This may appear as a separate section or may be integrated into the closing portion of your manuscript.
This section should address:
What remains incomplete or still developing
What you would do next if you continued this project
How the review could be extended, completed, or strengthened
This content is not an admission of failure. It demonstrates your awareness of the review process, the current state of your manuscript, and the realistic work that would be needed to continue developing the manuscript.
Expectations for Revision
Your final manuscript should reflect meaningful revision based on peer feedback, instructor feedback, writing support, and your own self-assessment. You are not expected to revise every part of the manuscript equally. Instead, focus on high-impact revisions that strengthen clarity, coherence, synthesis, organization, and alignment with your selected methodology.
Improve clarity and organization where readers may become confused
Strengthen synthesis and connections across sources
Develop key components, especially discussion, implications, limitations, or next steps where possible
Clarify how the manuscript aligns with your selected review methodology
Maintain a scholarly, professional voice throughout the manuscript
Submission Expectations
Submit the final manuscript in Word document format.
Include a title/cover page.
Identify and consistently apply the academic formatting style appropriate to your discipline (e.g., APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.).
Include all planned sections, even if some are still developing.
Include a minimum of 20 scholarly sources.
Expected length: 10+ pages, double-spaced, excluding title page and references.
If you use a source manager (e.g., Zotero, EndNote), submit a version with citations unlinked; keep a separate working version with active links.
Submit or maintain an updated working document if required by your instructor. All sources cited in the manuscript should be represented in your working document.
Evaluation Focus
Coherence and organization
Depth and quality of analysis appropriate to the selected methodology
Alignment between structure, analysis, and methodology
Scholarly voice and clarity
Strength of synthesis across sources
Development of discussion and implications, as appropriate to stage and methodology
Thoughtful articulation of limitations and next steps
Evidence of revision and responsiveness to feedback
