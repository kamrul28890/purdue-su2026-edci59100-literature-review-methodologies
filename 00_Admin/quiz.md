# Literature Review Methodologies Quiz

## Question 1
**Which review type is best for mapping the extent of research in a field?**
* Scoping
* Narrative
* Systematic
* Integrative

**Correct Answer:** Scoping  
*Reasoning:* Scoping reviews are specifically designed to "map" the existing literature—assessing the size, scope, and nature of the available research, often to identify gaps before a more focused review is conducted.

## Question 2
**Systematic reviews are always the best choice.**
* True
* False

**Correct Answer:** False  
*Reasoning:* There is no universally "best" review type. While systematic reviews are highly rigorous, they are designed to answer very narrow, specific questions. If your goal is to broadly explore an emerging topic or develop a theory, other methodologies (like scoping or theoretical reviews) are much better suited.

## Question 3
**Which review type uses the most structured and replicable process?**
* Scoping
* Integrative
* Narrative
* Systematic

**Correct Answer:** Systematic  
*Reasoning:* Systematic reviews follow strict, predefined protocols (such as PRISMA) detailing exact search terms, databases, and inclusion/exclusion criteria to minimize bias and ensure other researchers could replicate the exact same search.

## Question 4
**Which review type is MOST flexible in its search process?**
* Systematic
* Narrative
* Scoping
* Theoretical

**Correct Answer:** Narrative  
*Reasoning:* Narrative (or traditional) reviews often do not require a formal, documented search strategy, allowing the author flexibility to select studies that provide a comprehensive overview or historical context.

## Question 5
**Which review type is best for exploring a new or emerging topic?**
* Scoping
* Theoretical
* Systematic
* Narrative

**Correct Answer:** Scoping  
*Reasoning:* Because emerging topics may not have enough data for a systematic review or may lack defined boundaries, a scoping review is ideal for taking an initial look at what type of evidence currently exists.

## Question 6
**What should guide your choice of methodology?**
* Which is the most rigorous
* Instructor preference
* Fit for Purpose
* Which is the easiest

**Correct Answer:** Fit for Purpose  
*Reasoning:* The methodology must align directly with your research question and the overall goal of your study.

## Question 7
**Match each purpose to the correct review type:**

**Options:**
1. Identify patterns across diverse studies
2. Provide a broad overview of a topic
3. Develop or refine theory
4. Map the extent of research on a topic
5. Answer a focused research question using structured methods

**Correct Matches:**
* **Narrative:** 2. Provide a broad overview of a topic
* **Scoping:** 4. Map the extent of research on a topic
* **Systematic:** 5. Answer a focused research question using structured methods
* **Theoretical:** 3. Develop or refine theory
* **Integrative:** 1. Identify patterns across diverse studies  
*(Note: Integrative reviews are unique because they intentionally combine diverse methodologies—such as experimental and non-experimental research—to identify overarching patterns.)*

## Question 8
**A researcher wants to understand how a specific instructional strategy impacts student outcomes, and they plan to use a clearly defined search process with strict inclusion criteria. Which review type most closely aligns with this approach?**
* Systematic
* Theoretical
* Integrative
* Scoping
* Narrative

**Correct Answer:** Systematic  
*Reasoning:* The combination of a highly specific question ("how a specific instructional strategy impacts outcomes") and a "clearly defined search process with strict inclusion criteria" is the textbook definition of a systematic review.

## Question 9
**Which statement best distinguishes a scoping review from an integrative review?**
* Scoping reviews use strict inclusion criteria, while integrative reviews do not include empirical studies
* Scoping reviews are more detailed than integrative reviews
* Scoping reviews map the range of research, while integrative reviews focus on identifying patterns across studies
* Scoping reviews focus on theory, while integrative reviews focus on empirical studies

**Correct Answer:** Scoping reviews map the range of research, while integrative reviews focus on identifying patterns across studies  
*Reasoning:* Scoping reviews are about categorizing the *landscape* of available evidence, whereas integrative reviews synthesize diverse types of existing literature to create new perspectives or find underlying patterns.

## Question 10
**Which of the following represents a mismatch between purpose and methodology?**
* Using a scoping review to map research on an emerging topic
* Using a theoretical review to examine how a concept has developed
* Using a systematic review to explore a broad, undefined topic
* Using an integrative review to identify patterns across studies

**Correct Answer:** Using a systematic review to explore a broad, undefined topic  
*Reasoning:* Systematic reviews require narrow, highly specific research questions. Using one for a broad or undefined topic would result in an unmanageable amount of data and defeat the purpose of the methodology.

## Question 11
**A review includes: a broad research question, no clearly defined search process, a general summary of key studies. Which review type does this MOST closely resemble?**
* Systematic
* Integrative
* Theoretical
* Scoping
* Narrative

**Correct Answer:** Narrative  
*Reasoning:* These characteristics define a traditional narrative review, which is more conversational, broad, and less structured than systematic or scoping methods.

## Question 12
**Why is it acceptable to make an incorrect prediction when identifying a review type in this course?**
* Because it is not graded
* Because the goal is to use evidence and explain reasoning
* Because all review types are similar
* Because identifying methodology is subjective

**Correct Answer:** Because the goal is to use evidence and explain reasoning  
*Reasoning:* In academic methodology training, demonstrating that you understand the underlying concepts and can critically defend your choices using evidence is often more valuable than simply guessing the "correct" label.
